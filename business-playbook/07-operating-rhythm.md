# 07 — The operating rhythm

Three loops. Twenty minutes a day, one hour a week, two hours a month. Everything in this folder
fits inside them.

The point of writing them down is that a business run from a phone has no natural structure, and
without one the urgent work (replying) permanently crowds out the important work (knowing the
numbers, buying the right stock).

---

## Daily, 20 minutes

Split it. Ten minutes in the morning, ten at the end of the day.

**Morning**

- [ ] Clear `New enquiry` in WhatsApp. Every thread gets a real reply, not a holding message
- [ ] Post one story: a piece, a detail, a note, a reply to a question someone asked
- [ ] Check one label queue: `Sent details` if it is a follow-up day, `Sourcing` otherwise

**End of day**

- [ ] Log every sale into the Sales tab, **before closing the phone**
- [ ] Move every thread to the label that now describes it
- [ ] Reply to comments and DMs on Instagram and TikTok

That is the whole daily loop. If a day collapses, the one item that cannot slip is logging the
sale, because it is the only one that cannot be recovered later.

---

## Weekly, one hour, Monday morning

```
  0–10    NUMBERS        Sales last week, enquiries last week, anything reserved and unpaid
  10–20   STOCK          What arrived, what is nearly gone, what has not moved in 60 days
  20–45   CONTENT        Shoot or pull, build three tiles, write three captions
  45–55   SCHEDULE       Meta Business Suite for Mon / Wed / Fri, plus TikTok
  55–60   FOLLOW-UPS     Every thread on `Sent details` older than 48 hours. Once each
```

**Pick the three pieces before opening Canva.** Deciding what to post while designing is what turns
a one-hour batch into a three-hour afternoon. Pull them from the Inventory tab, and deliberately
include one slow mover each week: the posting schedule is also the merchandising schedule.

If the week is going badly, protect the batch and drop everything else. Three scheduled posts means
the next seven days happen without you.

---

## Monthly, two hours, first working day

**Part one: the numbers, 30 minutes.** Open the Dashboard.

- [ ] Revenue and margin, this month against last. Trend, not target
- [ ] Margin %. If it is falling, find out whether it is discounting or a change in mix
- [ ] Stock at cost. Is it growing faster than revenue? That is cash going the wrong way
- [ ] Top five by margin. These are the reorder candidates
- [ ] Channel breakdown. Where is effort actually paying
- [ ] Enquiry to sale %. Below 15% with healthy enquiries is a conversion problem, not a reach one

**Part two: dead stock, 20 minutes.** Work the over-90-days list and make a decision on **every**
row. Discount, bundle, gift-with-purchase, or return to supplier. Leaving it is also a decision and
it is the expensive one.

**Part three: buying, 30 minutes.** Reorder from the margin list, not the units list. Replace what
sold, and give yourself one deliberate experiment: a piece outside the usual range that tests
something. One, not five.

**Part four: content review, 20 minutes.** Look at the last month's posts sorted by saves and
shares. Two questions only: what format did the best three share, and what did the worst three
share? Do more of the first thing.

**Part five: housekeeping, 20 minutes.**

- [ ] Export the sheet: File → Download → Excel, into the Drive backup folder
- [ ] Photographs from the month backed up to Drive or iCloud
- [ ] Message the `Repeat customer` label about new arrivals. This is usually the highest-return
      twenty minutes of the month
- [ ] Check every account still has 2FA on and recovery codes stored

---

## Quarterly, half a day

Four times a year, step out of the loops.

- **Prices.** Cost prices move and list prices do not follow on their own. Check the margin column
  across the whole Inventory tab and correct anything that has drifted below target.
- **The website.** Is the catalog current? Are there placeholder rows left? Run
  `npm run catalog:check`, which prints the remaining placeholder count. Zero is the goal.
- **The collections.** Eight were confirmed in July 2026. After two quarters of real sales data, is
  the split still right? A collection with no sales is a candidate for retirement, and one with a
  waiting list is a candidate for depth.
- **Suppliers.** Who delivers reliably, whose margins are worst, who has become the dependency that
  would hurt if they stopped.
- **The photographs.** Re-shoot the best sellers. The first attempt at product photography is never
  the best one, and the top five pieces earn the re-shoot.

---

## What to do when it slips

It will. A week disappears, nothing gets posted, the sheet falls behind.

**Do not try to catch up.** Backfilling three weeks of posts is what makes people quit entirely.

1. Log the sales. Reconstruct from WhatsApp threads, which have timestamps and are usually enough.
2. Post one thing today. Not seven.
3. Resume Monday's batch as if nothing happened.

The system is designed to be resumed, not completed. A gap is a gap. A quiet apology post about the
gap is worse than the gap, because it draws attention to it and it is the kind of post that becomes
a habit.
